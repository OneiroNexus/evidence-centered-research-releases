# Research that can be found, checked and reused

**Aaron Michael Weis** · Oneiron Holdings LLC · [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-1906-148X)  
Concept paper · source draft dated 11 November 2026 · public research edition v0.1.0

## Abstract

A research release can be easy to find and difficult to use. This paper proposes following the reader through four distinct events: finding a work, understanding its claim, attempting its stated check, and using the result. It separates citation metadata from evidence about a result and illustrates why a projection of audience response needs actual observations. The release design is a proposal. It reports no traffic experiment or measured change in uptake.

## What happens after a reader finds a title

A reader who discovers a project needs enough information to make a second decision. Is this a concept, a working prototype, a reported experiment, or an independent replication? What version is being cited? What is the smallest result a stranger can check? A short README can answer those questions and link directly to the evidence. Omitting a known limitation makes the reader repeat work that the author could have documented once.

Citation metadata and an archived edition serve different parts of this process. A GitHub citation file supplies information about how to cite a work. Zenodo’s GitHub integration can preserve a tagged release and assign a persistent identifier. A version DOI gives a reader a destination. To verify a result, the reader still needs the relevant input, method and observation, or a clear explanation of any unavailable material. ## What would count as uptake?

Let *D* be qualified visits during a specified period. Let *q* be the fraction of those visits that produce a reproduction attempt, and *c* the fraction of attempts that produce a useful inquiry. Then *D × q × c* is the implied number of inquiries under that accounting model. The events must refer to the same audience, period and unit. Repeat visits and repeated attempts need an explicit counting rule.

With 1,000 assumed visits, a 2% attempt fraction and a 10% inquiry fraction, the calculation gives two inquiries. The figures illustrate the multiplication. They provide no estimate of any project’s audience, revenue or probability of success. A reader who attempts a check, finds a problem and never files an inquiry has still supplied an important observation if the failure is reported.

## An evaluation that could change the release

Ask an unfamiliar reader to find the stated claim, identify the archived version, locate the input and run the smallest documented check. Record where the reader stops, what they expected to happen, and the result they actually obtained. Repeat with a revised explanation and comparable readers or tasks. Differences in expertise, task difficulty and prior exposure can affect the comparison.

The author proposes reporting a reproduction attempt as its own event. Passing a local test is evidence about that run. An independent reproduction requires a separate attempt with identifiable inputs and outcomes. Useful reuse is a further event and may take much longer to observe. Reporting these stages separately allows a low-traffic release with a successful independent check to receive the credit its evidence warrants.
